Research & Literature

Research Papers.

Learn how to find, understand, read, and critically evaluate scientific literature as a biotechnology student.

What Is a Research Paper?

A research paper communicates the question, methods, results, and conclusions of a scientific investigation.

Why Should Students Learn to Read Papers?

Research papers introduce you to scientific work beyond textbooks. They can help you understand current discoveries, experimental techniques, evidence, and different scientific viewpoints.

Student tip: You do not need to understand every sentence on your first reading. Start by identifying the main question, methods, results, and conclusion.

Structure of a Research Paper

Most scientific papers follow a recognizable structure, although the exact format can vary between journals.

Abstract

A short summary of the research question, methods, major results, and conclusions.

Introduction

Provides background information and explains the scientific problem or research question.

Methods

Explains how the study was conducted, including experimental procedures and analysis methods.

Results

Presents the observations, measurements, analyses, figures, tables, and statistical findings.

Discussion

Interprets the results and explains their meaning in relation to previous research.

Conclusion

Summarizes the main findings and their significance.

References

Lists the scientific sources cited by the authors.

Figures & Tables

Present data visually and can make complex results easier to understand.

How to Read a Research Paper

You do not always need to read a scientific paper from beginning to end in a single pass.

1

Read the Title

Identify the main topic and determine whether the paper is relevant to your question.

2

Read the Abstract

Get a quick overview of the research question, approach, major findings, and conclusion.

3

Identify the Research Question

Ask what the researchers were actually trying to investigate or discover.

4

Look at the Figures and Tables

Examine the main data before getting lost in every detail of the text.

5

Check the Methods

Understand how the researchers collected their evidence and whether the methods fit the research question.

6

Read the Discussion

See how the authors interpret their findings and compare them with previous research.

7

Check the Conclusion

Identify the main message of the study and what the researchers believe their findings mean.

Original Research vs Review Articles

Knowing the difference helps you choose the right type of paper for your purpose.

Original Research Article

Presents new research conducted by the authors. It normally includes methods, newly collected or analyzed data, results, and interpretation.

Review Article

Summarizes and discusses findings from multiple existing studies rather than primarily presenting a new experiment.

Beginner tip: Review articles can be useful when you are first learning about an unfamiliar research topic.

Think Critically

A published paper is not automatically proof that every conclusion is correct. Consider the study design, sample size, controls, methods, results, limitations, and whether the conclusions are supported by the evidence.
