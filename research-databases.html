Research Resources

Research Databases.

Discover important biological databases that help students explore sequences, proteins, genomes, structures, and scientific literature.

Why Use Research Databases?

Biological databases organize scientific information so researchers can search, compare, analyze, and retrieve biological data.

From Textbooks to Real Research

Textbooks are useful for learning concepts, but research often requires access to real biological data. Databases allow students to explore sequences, proteins, genomes, structures, and publications.

Student tip: Start with the type of biological information you need, then choose a database designed for that purpose.

Essential Database Categories

Different databases provide different types of biological information.

🧬

DNA & RNA Sequences

Explore nucleotide sequences from genes and organisms for sequence analysis and research.

🧪

🧩

Explore three-dimensional structures of proteins, nucleic acids, and other molecules.

🌍

Explore genomes, genes, genomic regions, and other genomic information.

📄

Search scientific publications, research papers, reviews, and biomedical literature.

Important Databases to Know

These are useful starting points for biotechnology students.

🧬 NCBI

NCBI provides access to many biological resources, including nucleotide sequences, proteins, genomes, genes, and scientific literature.

  • Search DNA and RNA sequences
  • Explore genes and genomes
  • Find protein information
  • Search scientific literature
Good starting point: Use NCBI when you need to search for a gene, sequence, protein, or research article.

🧪 UniProt

UniProt is a major resource for protein sequence and functional information.

  • Search protein sequences
  • Study protein functions
  • Explore protein annotations
  • Compare protein information
Student use: If you are studying a particular protein, UniProt is a useful place to begin.

🧩 Protein Data Bank (PDB)

The Protein Data Bank provides access to three-dimensional structural information for biological macromolecules.

  • Explore molecular structures
  • View three-dimensional models
  • Study protein structures
  • Explore molecular interactions

📄 PubMed

PubMed is a widely used resource for searching biomedical and life-science research literature.

  • Find research papers
  • Search scientific topics
  • Find review articles
  • Explore previous research
Research tip: Use specific keywords instead of very broad searches.

🌍 Ensembl

Ensembl provides genome information and tools for exploring genes, genomic regions, variants, and other genomic features.

  • Explore genomes
  • Find genes and genomic regions
  • Explore genetic variants
  • Study genome annotations

📊 Gene Expression Resources

Gene expression resources can help researchers investigate how genes are expressed under different biological conditions, tissues, or experimental settings.

Remember: Always check the experimental design and metadata before interpreting gene expression data.

How to Choose a Database

Identify what type of biological information you need first.

Looking for a DNA Sequence?

Start with a nucleotide sequence resource. NCBI is a useful starting point for many sequence searches.

Looking for Protein Information?

UniProt is useful for protein sequences, functions, and annotations.

Looking for a 3D Structure?

The Protein Data Bank is a major resource for exploring molecular structures.

Looking for Research Papers?

PubMed is a useful starting point for searching biomedical and life-science literature.

Looking for Genome Information?

Resources such as Ensembl and NCBI can help you explore genomes, genes, and genomic regions.

Research Like a Scientist

Do not rely on a single database without checking the source and context of the information. Understand what the data represents before drawing conclusions.
