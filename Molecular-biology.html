Molecular Biology

Test your molecular biology knowledge through foundation, advanced, experimental, and interview-oriented questions.

Molecular Biology Practice

Work through the questions carefully. Focus on understanding the scientific reasoning behind each answer.

FOUNDATION

1. What is the primary role of DNA in a cell?

Correct Answer: A — To store hereditary genetic information
DNA stores the genetic instructions used by cells. These instructions can be copied during replication and expressed through processes such as transcription and translation.
Key concept: DNA is the major long-term information-storage molecule of cellular inheritance.
FOUNDATION

2. Which molecule normally carries a transcribed genetic message from DNA toward the ribosome?

Correct Answer: B — Messenger RNA (mRNA)
mRNA acts as an intermediary carrying information copied from a gene to the ribosome, where its sequence can be used during protein synthesis.
Key concept: DNA → RNA → protein is a central framework for understanding gene expression.
FOUNDATION

3. What is the process of producing an RNA molecule using DNA as a template called?

Correct Answer: C — Transcription
Transcription uses one DNA strand as a template for producing a complementary RNA molecule. RNA polymerase catalyzes the synthesis of the growing RNA strand.
Key concept: Transcription converts DNA sequence information into an RNA copy.
FOUNDATION

4. Which molecule directly carries amino acids to the ribosome during translation?

Correct Answer: A — tRNA
Transfer RNA molecules carry specific amino acids and use their anticodons to pair with complementary codons in mRNA during translation.
Key concept: tRNA connects nucleotide information in mRNA with the amino acids used to build proteins.
FOUNDATION

5. Which base is normally present in DNA but replaced by uracil in RNA?

Correct Answer: C — Thymine
DNA normally contains thymine, whereas RNA normally contains uracil in its place. Adenine, cytosine, and guanine occur in both DNA and RNA.
Key concept: DNA uses T; RNA normally uses U.
INTERMEDIATE

6. Why is DNA replication described as semiconservative?

Correct Answer: A — One parental strand and one new strand
During replication, the two parental strands separate and each serves as a template for a new complementary strand. Therefore, each resulting DNA double helix contains one old and one newly synthesized strand.
Key concept: Complementary base pairing allows each parental strand to guide synthesis of a new strand.
INTERMEDIATE

7. DNA polymerase extends a newly synthesized DNA strand in which direction?

Correct Answer: B — 5′ → 3′
DNA polymerase adds nucleotides to the available 3′ end of the growing strand. As a result, the new DNA strand is synthesized in the 5′ → 3′ direction.
Key concept: The direction of DNA synthesis explains why leading and lagging strand synthesis differ.
INTERMEDIATE

8. What is the main reason the lagging strand is synthesized discontinuously?

Correct Answer: A
The two DNA strands are antiparallel, but DNA polymerase can extend DNA only in the 5′ → 3′ direction. Therefore, synthesis on one template must occur in short segments that are later joined together.
Key concept: Okazaki fragments are a consequence of strand antiparallelism and the directionality of DNA synthesis.
INTERMEDIATE

9. What is the primary function of DNA ligase during replication?

Correct Answer: B
DNA ligase seals breaks between adjacent DNA fragments, particularly those produced during discontinuous synthesis of the lagging strand.
Key concept: Ligase helps convert separate DNA fragments into a continuous DNA strand.
INTERMEDIATE

10. Which enzyme is primarily responsible for separating the two DNA strands at a replication fork?

Correct Answer: B — Helicase
Helicase helps unwind the DNA double helix at the replication fork, exposing template strands that can then be copied.
Key concept: Replication requires coordinated action of helicase, primase, polymerase, ligase and other proteins.
ADVANCED

11. A researcher wants to amplify a specific DNA region from a small sample. Which technique is most directly suited for this purpose?

Correct Answer: A — PCR
PCR is designed to amplify a defined DNA region in vitro. Sequence-specific primers determine which region is amplified.
Key concept: PCR is widely used to obtain many copies of a selected DNA region for downstream analysis.
ADVANCED

12. Why are primers required in PCR?

Correct Answer: A
DNA polymerase requires an existing nucleic-acid strand with a suitable 3′ end to extend. PCR primers bind to complementary target sequences and provide those starting points.
Key concept: Primer binding contributes to PCR specificity because primers define the region that is amplified.
ADVANCED

13. A mutation changes one DNA base but does not change the encoded amino acid. What type of mutation can this be?

Correct Answer: A — Silent mutation
Because multiple codons can encode the same amino acid, a nucleotide substitution can sometimes leave the amino-acid sequence unchanged.
Key concept: A DNA sequence change does not automatically mean that the resulting protein sequence changes.
ADVANCED

14. What is the most likely consequence of inserting one nucleotide into a protein-coding region?

Correct Answer: A
Because codons are read in groups of three nucleotides, adding one nucleotide can shift the reading frame for downstream codons. The resulting protein can therefore be substantially altered.
Key concept: Insertions or deletions that are not multiples of three can produce frameshift effects.
ADVANCED

15. A gene is transcribed at a much higher level after a regulatory change. What does this most directly indicate?

Correct Answer: A
Increased transcription means more RNA is being produced from the gene. Gene regulation controls when and how strongly genes are expressed.
Key concept: Changes in gene expression can occur without changing the underlying DNA sequence.
ADVANCED

16. Which statement best explains why cells can produce different amounts of different proteins?

Correct Answer: A
Cells regulate gene expression so that different genes can be transcribed and translated with different efficiencies and at different times.
Key concept: Gene regulation allows cells to respond to their needs instead of producing every protein equally.
INTERVIEW & RESEARCH

17. During PCR, a researcher obtains no amplification in the sample but the positive control works. Which conclusion is most reasonable as a first interpretation?

Correct Answer: A
A successful positive control indicates that the PCR setup is capable of producing amplification. A failed sample therefore shifts attention toward sample quality, target sequence, primer-template compatibility, inhibitors, or sample-specific factors rather than immediately concluding that PCR itself cannot work.
Interview tip: Controls help you distinguish a general assay failure from a sample-specific problem.
INTERVIEW & RESEARCH

18. In an experiment studying gene expression, why might measuring RNA levels be useful?

Correct Answer: A
Measuring RNA abundance can provide information about gene transcription and expression patterns. However, RNA abundance does not necessarily equal protein abundance because regulation can also occur after transcription.
Interview tip: Distinguish transcriptional regulation from post-transcriptional and translational regulation.
INTERVIEW & RESEARCH

19. A researcher wants to determine whether a specific DNA fragment is present in a sample. Which approach is most directly suited to detect and amplify that target?

Correct Answer: A
PCR can selectively amplify a defined DNA region when suitable primers target sequences surrounding the region of interest. The resulting amplification can then be analyzed using an appropriate detection method.
Interview tip: Always connect the biological question with the molecule and technique you actually need to measure.
INTERVIEW & RESEARCH

20. Why is a negative control useful in a molecular biology experiment?

Correct Answer: A
A negative control is expected to lack the target signal. Unexpected amplification or signal in that control can indicate contamination, nonspecific reactions, or another source of background.
Interview tip: Controls are essential for interpreting whether an observed result is actually meaningful.
INTERVIEW & RESEARCH

21. A scientist finds that a mutation occurs in a non-coding regulatory region. Which statement is the most scientifically appropriate?

Correct Answer: A
Non-coding regions can contain regulatory elements involved in controlling gene expression. Therefore, a mutation outside a protein-coding region can still influence when or how strongly a gene is expressed.
Interview tip: Do not assume "non-coding" means "non-functional."
INTERVIEW & RESEARCH

22. A researcher compares two cell populations and finds that one has much more mRNA from a particular gene. What is the safest conclusion?

Correct Answer: A
Higher RNA abundance indicates a difference in RNA levels under the conditions tested. It does not automatically prove a proportional difference in protein abundance or a difference in DNA sequence.
Research tip: Keep conclusions proportional to what your experiment actually measured.
INTERVIEW & RESEARCH

23. Why is proofreading important during DNA replication?

Correct Answer: A
DNA replication is highly accurate, and proofreading mechanisms help detect and correct certain copying errors. This contributes to maintaining genome stability.
Research tip: High replication fidelity results from multiple coordinated mechanisms rather than one single process.
INTERVIEW & RESEARCH

24. During an interview, you are asked: "Why is molecular biology important in biotechnology?" Which answer is strongest?

Correct Answer: A
Molecular biology provides the mechanistic understanding of genetic information, gene expression, proteins, and molecular interactions. That knowledge supports many biotechnology applications, including genetic engineering, diagnostics, research, and biotechnology product development.
Interview tip: A strong interview answer connects scientific fundamentals with real-world biotechnology applications.
