Research Basics.

Learn how scientific research begins, how researchers develop questions, design experiments, collect evidence, and reach scientifically supported conclusions.

What Is Scientific Research?

Scientific research is a systematic process used to investigate questions, test ideas, generate evidence, and improve our understanding of the natural world.

Research Is More Than Finding Information

Research involves asking a meaningful question and using appropriate methods to collect and analyze evidence. In biotechnology, research can help scientists understand diseases, develop treatments, improve crops, study microorganisms, analyze genes, and solve many biological problems.

Biotechnology example: Suppose researchers want to investigate whether a particular compound affects the growth of bacteria. They would develop a research question, design an experiment, collect measurements, analyze the results, and determine whether the evidence supports their hypothesis.

Key Research Concepts

These concepts form the foundation of experimental research.

1. Research Question

A research question clearly states what the researcher wants to investigate. A strong question should be focused, understandable, and possible to investigate.

Example: Does increasing the concentration of a nutrient affect bacterial growth?

2. Hypothesis

A hypothesis is a testable prediction about what may happen in an experiment. It should be possible to support or reject it using evidence.

Example: Increasing nutrient concentration will increase bacterial growth up to an optimal concentration.

3. Variables

Variables are factors that can change during an experiment.

  • Independent variable: the factor deliberately changed by the researcher.
  • Dependent variable: the factor measured or observed.
  • Controlled variables: factors kept constant to make the experiment fair.
